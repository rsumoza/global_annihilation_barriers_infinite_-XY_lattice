\section{Motivation for the energy-space gradient}

\textbf{Comment.} Explain why the chosen Hilbert metric and flow have a
physical or mathematical role beyond functional-analytic convenience.

\textbf{Change.} Section 2 now contains a subsection entitled
``Dissipation metric and dynamical interpretation.'' It starts from the
Rayleigh dissipation potential
\[
 \mathcal R(v)=\tfrac12\sum_e|D_ev|^2=\tfrac12\nrm v_\X^2.
\]
Minimizing $\mathcal R(v)+DE_b(u)[v]$ gives the weak gradient equation
and the identity $\dot E_b=-\sum_e|D_e\dot u|^2$. The metric therefore
corresponds to bond dissipation of phase-difference velocities. The
subsection derives the finite-array current-conservation interpretation
for overdamped resistively shunted Josephson junctions, with equal shunt
conductances, fixed boundary phases, no injected current and no noise.
Equation (13) of Yoon--Choi--Kim (2000), already cited in the article,
supports this interpretation. Its additional fluctuating-twist
equations are explicitly excluded.

The infinite lattice is formulated directly in the energy space; the
revision does not assume a thermodynamic limit of a driven stochastic
circuit model. It also distinguishes the static variational barrier
from its choice of dissipative relaxation law. The same energy and path
class define the same barrier if mobility is changed, while orbit and
rate questions require additional analysis. The phenomenon certified
here is pinned-dipole annihilation and its positive escape barrier,
not a proof of the thermodynamic BKT phase transition.

\section{Effective localization and practical computation}

\textbf{Comment.} Identify specific cancellations or averaging methods
that could reduce the very large truncation budgets.

\textbf{Change.} Section 7 now separates the two cost mechanisms.
The proved error is $C_E/R$, so a target $\varepsilon/4$ requires
$R\ge4C_E/\varepsilon$. The present $C_E\simeq1.60\times10^{17}$
is large, but the dependence on inverse accuracy is linear.
The exponential obstruction is a complete cubical search in the
number of variables, itself of order $R^2$.

The concrete next steps are data-dependent core sums of the nonlinear
source and separate exterior bounds, followed by leading-multipole
subtraction before absolute values. An exact kernel-difference identity
shows where this cancellation would enter. An improved rate or useful
constant still requires quantitative kernel differences, source moments,
and uniform control along the path and admitted coupling class.
Neutral vortex charge alone does not establish the required moments or
their cancellations. No unproved multiphase averaging is presented as
a solution: it need not preserve real lifts, charges, or the energy
ceiling. The revision retains the low-rank atlas plus analytic exterior
as the practical architecture, without claiming a new feasible global
solver or a new barrier computation.

\section{Dependent bond angles in the perturbation bound}

\textbf{Comment.} Explain why compatibility constraints among bond angles
do not invalidate the lower and upper enclosures for $W_b$.

\textbf{Change.} The proof of Lemma 6.1 now writes the bondwise inequality
\[
 2\min(b_e,0)\le b_e(1-\cos D_eu)\le2\max(b_e,0).
\]
It holds for every phase field before summing. Absolute summability of
$b$ permits summing it on the infinite lattice. Hence the constraints
between increments cannot invalidate the enclosures. Their endpoints
need not be attainable minima or maxima; the exact width $2\nrm b_1$
of this enclosing interval is an upper bound on the actual oscillation.
No strict inequality or independent choice of bond angles is assumed.
